The Parthenon, Greece

Constructed: 447–432 BCE

Perched above Athens for 2,500 years, the Parthenon was built almost entirely without mortar, its marble blocks cut so precisely they lock together like a puzzle. Every column bulges slightly outward and leans inward by design, an optical trick that makes the whole temple appear perfectly straight from a distance. It has survived earthquakes, fires, an explosion that blew off its roof in 1687, and centuries of looting, yet it remains the defining symbol of ancient Greek civilization and the birthplace of Western architecture.

Type: Classical Greek Architecture
