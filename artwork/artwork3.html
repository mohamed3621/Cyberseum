Girl With A Pearl Earring

Created: c. 1665

Often called the 'Mona Lisa of the North,' Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring isn't a portrait of a specific person but what's known as a tronie, a study of an imagined face and expression. The pearl itself is likely not a real pearl at all, but a painted illusion of glass or tin, since a jewel that size would have been extraordinarily expensive for a working painter. Almost nothing is known about the model or why Vermeer painted her, and that mystery is part of what has kept people staring into her gaze for over 350 years.

Type: Dutch Golden Age Portrait
