A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte

Created: 1884–1886

Scene of Leisure and Light, known as A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte – Created by Georges Seurat

*A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte* is one of the most famous works of Georges Seurat and a landmark of the Pointillist technique. The painting depicts Parisians leisurely enjoying a sunny afternoon along the Seine River. Seurat used thousands of tiny dots of color to create a luminous, harmonious scene, showcasing his scientific approach to light and color. It stands as a defining masterpiece of Post‑Impressionism.

A calm yet meticulously constructed view of Parisian leisure, A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte (1884–1886) showcases Seurat’s pioneering use of pointillism — tiny dots of color that blend in the eye. The stillness of the figures, the glowing light, and the precise composition give the scene a timeless, almost dreamlike quality. It’s one of the defining works of Post‑Impressionism.

Type: Post‑Impressionist / Pointillist Painting
