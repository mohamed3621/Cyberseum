Lady With An Ermine

Created: c. 1489–1490

Portrait of a Noble Young Woman, known as Lady with an Ermine – Created by Leonardo da Vinci

*Lady with an Ermine* is a celebrated portrait by Leonardo da Vinci, depicting Cecilia Gallerani, a young noblewoman from Milan. The painting is admired for its lifelike detail, graceful pose, and the symbolic ermine she holds—representing purity, virtue, and possibly referencing her connection to Ludovico Sforza, Duke of Milan. Leonardo’s mastery of light, anatomy, and expression makes this one of the most refined portraits of the Italian Renaissance.

A refined and quietly powerful portrait from around 1489–1490, Lady with an Ermine shows Cecilia Gallerani, the young mistress of Ludovico Sforza, Duke of Milan. Leonardo captures her with striking realism — the smooth skin, alert gaze, and elegant pose — while the white ermine in her arms symbolizes purity, status, and her connection to the Duke. It’s one of Leonardo’s most intimate and expressive portraits.

Type: Renaissance Portrait Painting
