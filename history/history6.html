The Roman Empire

Period: 27 BCE – 476 CE (West)

At its height, the Roman Empire controlled roughly 5 million square kilometers and around 70 million people, connected by over 400,000 kilometers of roads, many of which still form the basis of modern European routes. Rome pioneered concrete strong enough that some of its structures, like the Pantheon's dome, have survived nearly 2,000 years without reinforcement, using a formula involving volcanic ash that modern engineers are still studying to improve today's construction materials. Its eventual fall in 476 CE wasn't a single collapse but a centuries-long decline of economic, political, and military pressure.

Type: Imperial Civilization
